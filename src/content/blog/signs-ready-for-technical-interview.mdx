You've been grinding LeetCode for three months. You can solve medium problems in 30 minutes. Your friend says you're ready. But are you really?

Applying too early wastes precious networking referrals and burns bridges with companies that won't re-interview you for 6-12 months. Waiting too long means missed opportunities and stagnant skills.

Here's how to know—objectively—when you're ready to start interviewing.

## TL;DR

**You're ready if:**
- You solve 70%+ of medium problems in 35 minutes without hints
- You can explain your solution out loud while coding
- You've passed 3+ peer mock interviews with strangers
- You know your resume inside-out and can defend every bullet point
- You're getting interviews (even rejections mean your resume works)

**You're NOT ready if:**
- You freeze when someone watches you code
- You can't explain time/space complexity without Googling
- You've never done a full mock interview with another person

## Sign #1: You Solve 70%+ of Medium Problems Without Hints ✅

**What "ready" looks like:**
Open a random LeetCode medium you've never seen. Set a 35-minute timer. Solve it completely—working code, handles edge cases, optimal solution—without looking at hints or discussions.

Your success rate should be 7+ out of 10. If you're at 4-5, you need more practice.

**Why this matters:**
Real interviews give you 30-45 minutes per problem. If you need hints or multiple attempts in practice, you'll fail under pressure.

### The Self-Assessment Test

Try these 5 random mediums (don't Google first):
1. [Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters](https://leetcode.com/problems/longest-substring-without-repeating-characters/)
2. [3Sum](https://leetcode.com/problems/3sum/)
3. [Course Schedule](https://leetcode.com/problems/course-schedule/)
4. [LRU Cache](https://leetcode.com/problems/lru-cache/)
5. [Binary Tree Level Order Traversal](https://leetcode.com/problems/binary-tree-level-order-traversal/)

**Scoring:**
- 4-5 solved in time: **Ready**
- 2-3 solved: **2-4 weeks more practice needed**
- 0-1 solved: **Not ready—focus on fundamentals first**

## Sign #2: You Can Explain While Coding ✅

**What "ready" looks like:**
You open a problem. Before writing code, you:
1. Restate the problem in your own words
2. Walk through 2-3 examples manually
3. Describe your approach before coding
4. Explain time/space complexity
5. Identify edge cases upfront

Then you code while narrating: "I'm initializing a hash map here to track seen values... Now I'm iterating through the array..."

**Why this matters:**
Silent coding is an instant red flag. According to a Google hiring study (2024), communication clarity matters as much as correctness in technical interviews.

**How to test yourself:**
Record yourself solving a problem out loud. Watch it back. Did you:
- ✅ Explain your thinking before typing?
- ✅ Narrate what you're doing as you code?
- ✅ Catch bugs by talking through the logic?
- ❌ Code silently then explain after?
- ❌ Say "umm" or "so yeah" every sentence?

If you can't explain fluently while coding, you're not ready for real interviews yet.

## Sign #3: You've Passed Multiple Stranger Mock Interviews ✅

**What "ready" looks like:**
You've completed at least 3-5 mock interviews with people you've never met—ideally recruiting for similar roles—and received "yes, I'd move you forward" feedback from 70%+ of them.

Not just friends who are easy on you. Strangers who judge like real interviewers.

**Why this matters:**
Coding in front of others triggers different anxiety than solo practice. If you haven't survived that pressure in low-stakes mocks, you'll freeze in high-stakes real interviews.

Research from Stanford's Career Center (2025) found that candidates who completed 5+ stranger mock interviews passed real phone screens at 2.6x the rate of those who only practiced solo.

### The Stranger Test Checklist

After each mock, ask yourself:
- Did I understand the question without multiple clarifications?
- Did I start coding within 5 minutes of hearing the problem?
- Did I finish a working solution in time?
- Did the interviewer say they'd "move me forward" if this were real?
- Did I handle follow-up questions without panicking?

If you're answering "no" to more than one, you need more practice.

## Sign #4: You Know Your Resume Better Than Your Interviewer ✅

**What "ready" looks like:**
For every single bullet point on your resume, you can:
- Describe the project context in 2 sentences
- Explain your specific contribution (not "we did X"—what did YOU do?)
- Cite exact numbers (users, revenue, time saved, percentage improvement)
- Answer "Why did you choose that approach?" and "What would you do differently now?"

**Why this matters:**
Behavioral rounds often start with "Walk me through your resume." If you stumble explaining your own experience, you've failed before the first real question.

### The Resume Interrogation Test

Pick a random bullet point. Set a timer for 3 minutes. Answer:
1. What was the business context? Why did this project matter?
2. What specifically did YOU do? (Not your team—you.)
3. What was the measurable outcome?
4. What did you learn?
5. What would you do differently now?

If you can't answer these confidently for EVERY bullet, you're not ready.

## Sign #5: You're Getting Interviews (Even If Rejections) ✅

**What "ready" looks like:**
You've applied to 15-20 jobs. You're getting at least 20-30% response rate for phone screens—even if you're failing them.

If you're getting interviews, your resume works. The issue is interview execution, not readiness to apply.

**Why this matters:**
Zero responses means your resume is the problem—fix that before practicing more LeetCode. Some responses mean you're ready to interview; you just need to convert better.

### Interpretation Guide

| Response Rate | What It Means | Action |
|---------------|---------------|--------|
| 0-10% callbacks | Resume/ATS issue | Fix formatting, keywords, tailor to JDs |
| 20-40% callbacks | Resume works, failing interviews | More mock interviews, focus on communication |
| 50%+ callbacks | Strong resume AND referrals working | Keep going, refine based on feedback |

**If you're not applying yet:**
You don't know if you're ready until you test the market. Apply to 5 "practice companies" you don't care about. Real interview feedback > speculation.

## Warning Sign #1: You Freeze When Someone Watches ❌

**What this looks like:**
Solo, you solve problems confidently. The moment someone observes—peer, mock interviewer, real recruiter—you forget syntax, second-guess yourself, or go silent.

**Why you're not ready:**
Real interviews are observed by definition. If you can't perform under observation, you can't pass interviews.

### How to Fix It

**Week 1-2: Build comfort with observation**
- Pair program with a friend on easy problems (don't interview yet, just code together)
- Stream yourself solving problems on Discord/Twitch (audience of zero is fine—knowing you COULD be watched helps)

**Week 3-4: Gradual pressure exposure**
- Do 2-3 mock interviews with supportive peers who know you
- Focus on "finishing something working" rather than optimal solutions

**Week 5+: Real simulation**
- Do mock interviews with strangers on MatchMyInterview
- Ask them to be "realistic, not harsh" at first
- Once comfortable, ask for brutally honest feedback

## Warning Sign #2: You Can't Articulate Complexity Without Googling ❌

**What this looks like:**
Interviewer asks: "What's the time complexity of your solution?"

You: "Uhh... I think it's O(n)? Or maybe O(n log n)? Let me check my notes..."

**Why you're not ready:**
Complexity analysis is non-negotiable in technical interviews. If you can't explain it on the spot, you don't understand your own code well enough.

### How to Fix It

**For every problem you solve, force yourself to answer:**
1. What's the time complexity? Why?
2. What's the space complexity? Why?
3. Could this be optimized further? How?
4. What's the best possible time complexity for this problem class? (Can't sort faster than O(n log n), can't search unsorted array faster than O(n), etc.)

**Memorize these patterns:**

| Pattern | Time | Space | Example |
|---------|------|-------|---------|
| Single loop | O(n) | O(1) | Sum of array |
| Nested loop | O(n²) | O(1) | Bubble sort |
| Divide and conquer | O(n log n) | O(log n) | Merge sort |
| Hash map lookup | O(1) avg | O(n) | Two sum |
| Recursion depth | O(n) stack | O(n) | DFS on tree |

Practice saying these out loud until they're automatic: "This is O(n) time because we iterate through the array once, and O(n) space because in the worst case, the hash map stores all n elements."

## Warning Sign #3: You've Never Done a Full Mock Interview ❌

**What this looks like:**
You've solved 200 LeetCode problems solo. You've read "Cracking the Coding Interview." But you've never sat through a full 45-minute technical interview with another person asking questions, interrupting, and probing edge cases.

**Why you're not ready:**
Real interviews involve:
- Clarifying vague requirements
- Handling interruptions mid-thought
- Answering follow-ups you didn't prepare for
- Debugging under time pressure while someone watches
- Justifying design decisions on the spot

None of that happens when you practice alone.

### How to Fix It

**This week: Do your first full mock interview**
1. Find a peer on MatchMyInterview or ask a friend recruiting for similar roles
2. Agree on format: 5min intro, 35min coding problem, 5min your questions
3. Treat it like a real interview: dress normally, use video, no pausing
4. Get feedback: "If this were real, would you move me forward? Why or why not?"

**Next two weeks: Do 3 more mocks with different people**
- Each person will have different interview styles
- Some will hint, others won't
- Some will interrupt, others will let you struggle
- You need exposure to all styles

**Before applying to dream jobs: Complete 5+ mocks with strangers**
Friends are too forgiving. Strangers will tell you the truth.

## The Trap of Over-Preparation: When "Ready" Becomes "Stagnant"

There's a flip side: some candidates never feel ready. They solve 500 problems, do 20 mocks, and still think "just one more week."

**Signs you're over-preparing:**
- You've been practicing 6+ months without applying
- You can solve hard problems but haven't interviewed anywhere
- You're re-doing problems you've already mastered
- You're waiting to feel "100% confident" (that never comes)

**Reality check:** You'll never feel fully ready. Interviews are unpredictable. At some point, you have to start applying and learn from real feedback.

### The 80% Rule

If you hit 4 out of 5 "ready" signs, start applying. You'll learn more from 5 real phone screens than 100 more solo LeetCode problems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many problems should I solve before I'm ready?
There's no magic number. Some people are ready after 100 problems, others need 300. Focus on success rate (70%+ mediums) and consistency, not raw volume.

### Should I master hard problems before interviewing?
No. Most companies (except FAANG) rarely ask hards. Focus on solving mediums quickly and cleanly. Hards are useful for FAANG prep only.

### What if I'm ready technically but terrified of interviewing?
Fear is normal. But it only goes away through exposure, not more solo practice. Do 5 stranger mocks—by the third one, the fear diminishes significantly.

### Can I be ready for behavioral interviews even if my technical skills are weak?
No. Behavioral and technical readiness go together. If you can't pass technical screens, you'll never get to behavioral rounds. Fix technical first.

### How do I know if a mock interview "counts" as realistic?
It counts if: (1) the interviewer is a stranger or near-stranger, (2) they give honest feedback, (3) you treated it like a real interview (no pausing/redos).

### What if I fail my first real interview?
Expected. Most people fail their first 2-3. The goal isn't to pass everything—it's to learn what you don't know, then iterate. Treat first interviews as "paid practice."

## The Bottom Line: Readiness Is Earned, Not Felt

You'll never feel 100% ready. Confidence comes from surviving real interviews, not from endless preparation.

But you CAN know objectively if you're ready:
- ✅ 70%+ medium success rate
- ✅ Fluid communication while coding
- ✅ Passed stranger mocks
- ✅ Resume mastery
- ✅ No freeze under observation

Hit 4-5 of these? Start applying. Learn from real feedback. Iterate fast.

Waiting for perfection is procrastination in disguise.
